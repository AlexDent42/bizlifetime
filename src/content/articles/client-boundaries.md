Good boundaries are part of good service. They help clients understand what to expect and help a business deliver work consistently. The most useful boundaries are stated early, applied fairly, and explained in terms of how the work gets done.

## Define what is included

In a proposal or welcome note, describe the work, the deliverables, how many review rounds are included, and what would count as a new request. Specific examples can prevent different interpretations later.

## Agree on timing and communication

Tell clients when you are generally available, how quickly you respond during working days, and which channel to use for urgent matters. Set expectations that you can actually keep.

## Raise scope changes early

When a new request appears, acknowledge it and explain its effect on timing or cost. Offer a clear choice: replace something already planned, add time and budget, or schedule the extra work for later.

## Keep the tone calm and direct

You do not need a long apology to explain a limit. A simple message such as “I can add that to the project. It will need an additional two days, and I will send a revised estimate today” is respectful and clear.

## Make exceptions deliberately

Sometimes a small extra effort is worth making. Decide consciously, note what changed, and avoid turning one exception into an unspoken permanent commitment.

Boundaries work best when they support a reliable relationship. Clients know what is happening, and the business can plan its work without relying on last-minute adjustments.
