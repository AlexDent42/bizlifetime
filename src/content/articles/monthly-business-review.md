A monthly review does not need a stack of reports. Thirty focused minutes can help you step back from daily tasks and notice patterns worth acting on. Schedule it at the same point each month and keep notes from one review to the next.

## Minutes 1 to 5: Check the basics

Review sales or revenue, cash position, outstanding invoices, and major upcoming costs. Compare with the previous month and with your expectations. Look for questions to investigate rather than judging one month in isolation.

## Minutes 6 to 12: Notice customer signals

Which customers returned, referred someone, or gave useful feedback? What questions or complaints appeared more than once? Did any service or product take more effort than expected?

## Minutes 13 to 19: Review your time and operations

What work went smoothly? What had to be repeated, corrected, or delayed? Note one recurring task that could be clarified, simplified, or documented.

## Minutes 20 to 25: Compare plans with reality

Which planned outcome did you complete? What changed? Was the change caused by a reasonable new priority, an estimate that was too optimistic, or a process that needs attention?

## Minutes 26 to 30: Choose next steps

Write down up to three actions for the coming month. Give each action an owner and a next date. Also note what you are deliberately not taking on yet.

The review is useful when it leads to a small decision or a better question. Keep it brief enough that you will repeat it, and adapt the prompts to the way your own business works.
