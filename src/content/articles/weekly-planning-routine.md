Planning does not have to take an entire morning. A useful weekly routine can fit into 20 to 30 minutes and help you begin the week with a clearer picture of the work ahead.

## 1. Review what is already promised

Look at appointments, deadlines, deliveries, and client commitments. Check whether anything has moved and whether someone is waiting for information from you. Put the fixed commitments on the calendar first.

## 2. Check the money coming in and going out

Review invoices due, expected payments, and bills or subscriptions scheduled for the next couple of weeks. This is not a replacement for bookkeeping. It is a quick check for timing problems you can address early.

## 3. Choose three important outcomes

Write down the three results that would make the week feel well spent. Phrase them as outcomes rather than vague activities. “Send the revised proposal to Jordan” is easier to finish than “work on sales.”

If you have more than three priorities, decide what can wait. A priority list is useful only when it helps you make trade-offs.

## 4. Make room for the work

Add a realistic block of time for each priority. Leave open space for customer questions, routine admin, and unexpected work. If the calendar is already full, move or renegotiate something before adding more.

## 5. End with a quick reset

Before you finish, note the first task you will start next week and anything you need to prepare for it. That small handoff makes it easier to return to work without reconstructing your thoughts from scratch.

The routine should serve your business, not become another obligation. Keep the parts that help you see the week clearly and adjust the process when your workload changes.
