Planning does not have to take an entire morning or involve a complicated system. A short weekly review can help a small business owner see what is already promised, what needs attention, and where there is room to do the work. Set aside 20 to 30 minutes at a time you can usually protect, such as Friday afternoon or Monday morning.

## 1. Review what is already promised

Look at appointments, deadlines, deliveries, and client commitments. Check whether anything has moved and whether someone is waiting for information from you. Put fixed commitments on the calendar first, including preparation and travel time when those apply. If two promises now conflict, contact the people affected early and agree on a workable next step.

## 2. Check the money coming in and going out

Review invoices due, expected payments, and bills or subscriptions scheduled for the next couple of weeks. Note any invoice that needs a follow-up and any upcoming expense that needs to be planned for. This is not a replacement for bookkeeping or a full cash-flow forecast. It is a quick look ahead so a timing issue is less likely to surprise you.

## 3. Choose three important outcomes

Write down up to three results that would make the week feel well spent. Phrase each as an outcome rather than a vague activity. “Send the revised proposal to Jordan” is easier to finish than “work on sales.” A result may be serving a customer, completing an important admin task, or making progress on a longer-term improvement.

If everything seems important, decide what can wait, be delegated, or be reduced in scope. A priority list is useful when it helps you make trade-offs; it is not a promise that every item will fit.

## 4. Make room for the work

Add a realistic block of time for each priority. Break a large outcome into a first step that can fit into an available block. Leave open space for customer questions, routine administration, and unexpected work. If the calendar is already full, move or renegotiate something before adding more. Avoid planning every hour: a schedule with no margin can fall apart as soon as one task takes longer than expected.

## 5. End with a quick reset

Before you finish, note the first task you will start and anything you need to prepare for it. Put the relevant document, contact details, or materials somewhere easy to find. This small handoff makes it easier to begin without reconstructing your thoughts from scratch.

## A quick weekly review template

- What have I promised, and what changed?
- Which payments, invoices, or bills need attention soon?
- What are the one to three outcomes that matter most?
- When will I make progress on them, and what can wait?
- What is the first step I will take when I return?

Keep the review simple enough to repeat. If a section does not help you make a decision, shorten or remove it. The routine should give you a useful view of the week, not become another obligation competing for time.
